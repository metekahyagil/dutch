## 5. Grammar

Introduce the grammar concept. This section should:

- Present the concept simply
- Show the pattern
- Use examples
- Avoid overwhelming detail

Reference external grammar files when they exist.

```markdown
## Grammar

In Dutch, we have seven personal pronouns. They represent who is doing the action.

| Dutch | English | Usage |
|-------|---------|-------|
| ik | I | When you're talking about yourself |
| jij | you (informal) | When you're talking to one person casually |
| u | you (formal) | When you're talking to someone formally |
| hij | he | When you're talking about a man or masculine noun |
| zij | she | When you're talking about a woman or feminine noun |
| het | it | When you're talking about a neuter noun |
| wij | we | When you're talking about a group including yourself |
| zij | they | When you're talking about multiple people |

**See:** `/shared/grammar/personal-pronouns.md` for a complete explanation.
```

---

## 6. Grammar Reasoning

Explain WHY Dutch works this way. Connect it to patterns, English, or logic.

```markdown
## Grammar Reasoning

Dutch pronouns are similar to English, but with important differences:

- **Dutch is gendered:** Not just "he/she," but masculine/feminine/neuter
- **Dutch distinguishes formality:** "jij" (casual) vs "u" (formal)
- **Dutch plurals are simpler:** All plural pronouns use "zij"

This might feel complex, but Dutch speakers learned it naturally as children. You're learning the pattern; they learned the sound. It's easier than you think.
```

## 8. Pronunciation

Explain how the concepts in this lesson sound. Include audio descriptions.

```markdown
## Pronunciation

Dutch pronouns are pronounced clearly. Here's a guide:

- **ik** (ikh) -- Like the "k" at the end of "back"
- **jij** (yay) -- Like the letter "J" in English
- **u** (oo) -- Like the "oo" in "food"
- **hij** (hay) -- Like the "ai" in "wait"
- **zij** (zay) -- Like the "z" in "zoom" + "ay"
- **het** (het) -- Like "het" in English
- **wij** (way) -- Like the "ai" in "wait"

Listen to native speakers online to get the rhythm right.
```

## 14. Common Mistakes

Identify mistakes learners typically make and explain why they're mistakes.

```markdown
## Common Mistakes

1. **Confusing "jij" and "u"**
   - **Wrong:** Wat doet jij? (to your boss)
   - **Right:** Wat doet u? (to your boss)
   - **Why:** Use "jij" with friends/family. Use "u" with people you don't know well or in formal situations.

2. **Forgetting gendered pronouns**
   - **Wrong:** Ik zei Maria. Ik ben voorzichtig. (I said Mary. I am careful.) [using wrong pronoun]
   - **Right:** Ik ben Maria. Zij is voorzichtig.
   - **Why:** "Zij" (she) is for women/feminine nouns. "Hij" (he) is for men/masculine nouns.

3. **Using English word order with Dutch pronouns**
   - This will come later, but don't force English patterns onto Dutch.
```

## 15. Dutch Insight

A cultural or linguistic insight that helps the learner understand Dutch better.

```markdown
## Dutch Insight

Dutch speakers are generally informal. Many Dutch people use "jij" with colleagues, classmates, and even people they just met. The formal "u" is becoming less common in everyday speech. 

This doesn't mean you should use "jij" indiscriminately--it's still respectful to use "u" until someone suggests otherwise--but know that Dutch culture values directness and informality. You'll hear "jij" much more often than "u" in Dutch cities.
```

## 16. Memory Tricks

Memory aids to help learners remember this concept.

```markdown
## Memory Tricks

1. **Think of English:** Dutch pronouns are similar to English. "Ik" sounds like it means something; it does (I). "Wij" sounds like "we"; it is.

2. **The IJ combination:** Notice that "ij" makes a long "i" sound (like the English "i" in "ice"). This appears in "ik," "jij," and "wij."

3. **Formal vs. Informal:** You vs. you = jij vs. u. When in doubt, be formal. Dutch people will tell you to relax.
```
