# Research Request: A1-02 - Describing Daily Routines

## Competency

**Name:** Describing Daily Routines
**CEFR Level:** A1
**Lesson ID:** A1-02
**Prerequisite:** A1-01 (Basic Sentences and Word Order)

## Competency Description

The learner can:
- Describe everyday activities and routines using present-tense verbs
- Use adverbs of frequency (altijd, soms, nooit, meestal) to express habits
- Use time expressions to organize and sequence daily events (eerst, daarna, later, om [time], op [day])
- Ask about and discuss other people's daily routines
- Move from simple sentence construction (A1-01) to extended discourse describing a sequence of activities
- Use verb conjugation productively in a familiar, contextual domain (daily life)

## What We Need for This Lesson

For the A1-02 lesson, please research and provide:

### 1. Grammar Concepts

- **Review of Present Tense Conjugation:** Briefly recap the three-form system (stem, stem + -t, infinitive) in the context of describing routines
- **Adverbs of Frequency:** Explain how adverbs like altijd (always), soms (sometimes), nooit (never), meestal (usually), zelden (seldom) modify verb phrases; where they appear in the sentence (typically after the verb); how they describe habits and repeated actions
- **Time Expressions with Prepositions:** Explain om (at a clock time, e.g., om negen uur = at 9 o'clock), op (on a day, e.g., op maandag = on Monday), in (in a month/season, e.g., in januari = in January), per (per week = per week), and how these are used with adverbs of frequency
- **Sequencing and Narrative Adverbs:** Introduce eerst (first), daarna (afterwards/then), later (later), eindelijk (finally), voor (before), na (after) to help structure a description of daily events in order
- **Verb Forms for Routines:** Highlight that present tense in Dutch describes both single actions and habitual routines; no separate "habitual aspect" is needed (unlike some languages)
- **Variation with Regular Verbs:** Show that the conjugation rule from A1-01 applies to verbs like eten (to eat), slapen (to sleep), werken (to work), studeren (to study), etc., enabling learners to describe a wider range of daily activities

**Format for each concept:**
- Clear explanation
- Why it matters for describing daily life
- 4-6 examples with Dutch sentences and English translations
- Common mistakes Dutch learners make (e.g., word order with adverbs, misplacement of frequency adverbs, confusing om/op/in)

### 5. Exercises (Describe What Should Be Covered)

What types of exercises should this lesson include?

Suggested exercise types (choose/describe 5-8):
- **Verb Conjugation with Context:** Given a pronoun and a daily activity verb, conjugate it and use it in a sentence about routine
- **Adverb Placement:** Place an adverb of frequency (altijd, soms, nooit) in the correct position in given sentences
- **Sequence Sentences:** Use sequencing adverbs (eerst, daarna, later) to order scrambled sentences describing a daily routine
- **Fill-in Time Expressions:** Complete sentences with the correct preposition (om, op, in, per) and time word
- **Routine Description:** Write or speak a description of your morning/evening routine using 5-8 sentences with at least 3 different verbs and adverbs of frequency
- **Listening Comprehension:** Hear descriptions of daily routines and answer questions about the sequence or frequency of activities
- **Frequency Matching:** Match frequency adverbs to their correct English meanings and usage contexts
- **Reading Comprehension:** Read a personal description of someone's daily routine and answer comprehension questions
- **Speaking Practice:** Describe your own routine in response to a prompt, using sequencing adverbs and frequency expressions

**For each exercise type:**
- What skill it targets
- Approximate quantity (number of practice items or duration)
- One detailed example

### 6. Assessment Criteria

How will we know if a learner has mastered this lesson?

- Can describe their own daily routine in 6-10 sentences with correct verb conjugation, adverb placement, and appropriate time expressions
- Can accurately use and place adverbs of frequency (altijd, soms, nooit) in statements about habits
- Can sequence events using eerste, daarna, later, eindelijk, and similar sequencing adverbs
- Can answer questions about another person's daily routine with grammatically correct sentences
- Can identify and correct errors in frequency adverb placement and time expression usage
- Sample assessment tasks: provide 2-3 concrete tasks (e.g., "Write or speak your morning routine from wake-up to arriving at work/school. Use at least 5 different verbs, at least 3 time expressions, and at least 2 frequency adverbs.")
