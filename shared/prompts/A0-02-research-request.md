# Research Request: A0-02 - Introducing Yourself

## Competency

**Name:** Introducing Yourself  
**CEFR Level:** A0  
**Lesson ID:** A0-02

## Competency Description

The learner can:
- Say their name
- Ask someone's name
- State their age
- Say where they are from
- State their nationality
- Say which languages they speak
- Say where they live
- Give basic personal information

## What We Need for This Lesson

For the A0-02 lesson, please research and provide:

### 1. Grammar Concepts

Identify and explain the grammar concepts needed for this competency:

- **Personal pronouns** (ik, jij, hij, zij, het, u, we, zij)
- **Present tense of "zijn" (to be)** -- ik ben, jij bent, hij/zij/het is, wij zijn, jullie zijn, zij zijn
- **Present tense of "hebben" (to have)** -- ik heb, jij hebt, hij/zij/het heeft, wij hebben, jullie hebben, zij hebben
- **Simple yes/no questions** -- word order for questions (e.g., "Bent jij...?")
- **Possessive pronouns** (mijn, jouw, zijn, haar, ons, jullie, hun)
- **Negation with "niet"** -- placement and use

**Format:** For each grammar concept, provide:
- Clear explanation (as if teaching an English speaker who knows A0-01)
- Why it matters
- 3-5 examples with English translation
- Common mistakes English speakers make
- Contrast with A0-01 (what's new)

### 2. Vocabulary

25-35 words for this competency, organized by category:

- **Common Dutch/International first names** (mix of male, female, traditional, modern) -- at least 6 names
- **Countries and nationalities** (Netherlands, Belgium, Germany, France, Spain, England, USA, Poland, Romania, Turkey) -- at least 10 country/nationality pairs
- **Languages** (Dutch, English, German, Spanish, French, Polish, Turkish, etc.) -- at least 5 languages
- **Basic family relationships** (mother, father, brother, sister, married) -- at least 4 family words
- **Numbers 0-20** (for stating ages) -- all 21 numbers
- **Politeness and greeting phrases** (nice to meet you, my name is, what is your name, etc.) -- at least 3 phrases

**For each word/phrase, provide:**
- Dutch spelling
- English translation/meaning
- Article (de, het, or -- if not a noun)
- Plural form (or -- if not applicable)
- English approximation of pronunciation
- Part of speech
- One example sentence in Dutch
- English translation of the example

### 3. Example Sentences and Dialogues

Provide 3-4 short, realistic dialogues showing:
- How to introduce yourself
- How to ask someone's name
- How to state where you're from
- How to mention languages you speak
- How to mention family

**Format:** Dutch dialogue → English translation

**Constraint:** Use only present tense of "zijn" and "hebben," personal pronouns, and vocabulary from A0-01 + A0-02.

### 4. Cultural Notes

- **Dutch naming conventions** (first names, formal vs informal use)
- **Dutch attitudes toward language** (English speakers, multilingualism)
- **Dutch formality norms** -- when to use "jij" vs "u"
- **Geographic/demographic context** -- why certain countries matter (neighbors, economic partners, migration patterns)

### 5. Exercises (Describe What Should Be Covered)

What types of exercises should this lesson include? Describe:

- Dialogue practice (role-play introducing yourself)
- Question formation (ask "What's your name?")
- Pronoun matching
- Fill-in-the-blank (present tense of "zijn"/"hebben")
- Scrambled words (build a sentence)
- Listening comprehension (identify personal info from dialogue)
- Writing (write a self-introduction)

**For each exercise type:**
- What skill it targets
- Approximate quantity
- One example

### 6. Assessment Criteria

How will we know if a learner has mastered this lesson?

- Can maintain a 1-2 minute introduction conversation
- Can state: name, age, nationality, language(s) spoken, where from
- Can ask these questions back
- Can use "zijn" and "hebben" correctly in context
- Can use correct pronoun forms

## Guidelines

- Assume learner completed A0-01 (knows alphabet, vowel sounds, diphthongs, stress)
- Build on A0-01 vocabulary (no duplication)
- Use only present tense verbs (no future, no past)
- Keep sentences simple: subject + verb + object
- Avoid advanced grammar (no conditional, no complex subordination)
- All pronunciation in English approximation (no IPA symbols)
- Stress marks with UPPERCASE (e.g., NAHM, BEN-ted)
- Examples should be real Dutch and names common in Netherlands
- Dialogues should sound natural and practical
- Cultural notes should help English speakers understand Dutch social norms
