Teaching Philosophy ​

Language is a system, not a collection of isolated rules.

Students should understand the system rather than memorize facts.

Core Principle: Explain the Why ​

Every concept should answer these questions:

  • What? -- What is this thing?
  • Why? -- Why does Dutch work this way?
  • How? -- How do you use it practically?
  • When? -- When do you use it (vs. alternatives)?
  • Common mistakes? -- What do learners typically get wrong?
  • Relation to English? -- How does it compare to what I already know?

Example: Articles (de/het) ​

Wrong Way (Memorization) ​

"Dutch has two articles: de and het. Memorize which words use which."

Result: Students memorize lists and forget them.

Right Way (System) ​

"Dutch articles make distinctions English doesn't. Here's why:

  • Most words use de (about 70%)
  • Some words use het (about 30%)
  • Diminutives always use het
  • Infinitives use het
  • Neuter words (things without gender) use het
  • Here's a pattern you can use...
  • Here's what makes it hard...
  • Don't worry, even Dutch people look these up"

Result: Students understand the system and can make educated guesses.

Progressive Learning ​

Nothing is assumed before it's explicitly taught.

The Chain ​

  1. A0-01: Personal pronouns (I, you, he, she, it, we, they)
  2. A0-02: Present tense of "to be" (am, are, is)
  3. A0-03: Articles (de/het)
  4. A0-04: Regular verbs (present tense)

A lesson about articles never uses irregular verbs that haven't been taught.

A lesson about possessives never uses articles that haven't been explained.

Every Lesson States Its Prerequisites ​

## Prerequisites

Before this lesson, you should know:
- Personal pronouns (A0-01)
- Present tense of "to be" (A0-02)
- Articles (A0-03)

Learners can check: "Do I know all these? Yes? Let's go. No? Go back and review."

One Concept Per Lesson ​

A lesson introduces one major grammar concept.

Not:

  • "Articles and Plurals and Adjectives"

But:

  • Lesson 1: Articles
  • Lesson 2: Plurals
  • Lesson 3: Adjectives with articles
  • Lesson 4: Adjectives with plurals

Why? ​

  • Clear learning objectives
  • Cognitive load is manageable
  • Easier to review
  • Easier to assess mastery
  • Easier to fix if something isn't understood

High-Frequency First ​

Teach the most useful words first.

The 1,000 most common Dutch words cover about 90% of everyday conversation.

We teach:

  1. Core verbs (be, have, do, go, make, etc.)
  2. Pronouns and demonstratives
  3. Common prepositions (in, on, at, with, to, from)
  4. Essential adjectives (big, small, good, bad, hot, cold)
  5. Essential nouns (person, thing, place, time)
  6. Topic-specific vocabulary (family, food, transportation)

Not:

  • Rare technical terms
  • Archaic words
  • Obscure regional vocabulary
  • Things learners will never need

Active Recall and Spaced Repetition ​

Every lesson includes:

  • Immediate practice -- Exercise right after learning
  • Review within a lesson -- Recap and practice
  • Review lessons -- Every 5 lessons, review everything
  • Spaced reviews -- Come back to old lessons
  • Active recall -- Prompt the learner to generate answers, not recognize them

Why? ​

Memory requires effort. We optimize for retention:

  • Most learning happens during review, not during lessons
  • Spaced repetition is more effective than cramming
  • Active recall (testing yourself) is more effective than re-reading
  • Retrieval strengthens memory

Reuse and Reference ​

Grammar explanations are written once, referenced many times.

Why?

  • Consistency -- All lessons explain the same grammar the same way
  • Maintainability -- Update grammar once, it's fixed everywhere
  • Reduced cognitive load -- Learners see consistent patterns
  • Efficiency -- Don't duplicate work

Mistakes Are Data ​

Common mistakes should be collected, analyzed, and addressed.

Every grammar explanation includes a "Common Mistakes" section.

Why?

  • Learners make predictable mistakes
  • Addressing them prevents them
  • It shows the learner we understand how language learning works
  • It builds confidence ("I'm not stupid, this is just hard")

Comparison to English ​

Learners are native English speakers. Use that.

English: "I go, you go, he goes, we go, they go"

Dutch: "ik ga, jij gaat, hij gaat, wij gaan, zij gaan"

Insight: Dutch conjugates the same way as English, but with different sound patterns. The system is the same.

Why?

  • Reduces cognitive load (learners can build on what they know)
  • Shows that Dutch isn't arbitrary
  • Builds confidence
  • Helps learners understand why Dutch works

Reduce Cognitive Load ​

Every explanation should be as simple as possible.

Simple Language ​

  • Use short sentences
  • Use common words
  • Avoid jargon (or explain it first)
  • Use examples generously
  • Use analogies to things learners know

Scaffolding ​

  • Start with simple examples
  • Gradually add complexity
  • Show the pattern before exceptions
  • Introduce exceptions carefully

Not Overwhelming ​

  • One concept per lesson
  • 20-35 new vocabulary items per lesson
  • Exercises should feel manageable, not impossible
  • Success builds confidence

Learning Goals ​

By the end of each level, the learner should be able to:

A0:

  • Read Dutch with confidence
  • Introduce themselves
  • Ask and answer basic questions
  • Build simple sentences
  • Understand core grammar concepts

A1:

  • Conduct basic conversations
  • Discuss everyday topics (family, food, home)
  • Understand and respond to simple requests
  • Read simple texts
  • Write simple messages

A2:

  • Handle practical situations (shopping, healthcare, work)
  • Discuss opinions and experiences
  • Read and understand simple news
  • Write emails and longer messages
  • Understand conversations at normal speed

B1:

  • Discuss complex topics and opinions
  • Understand most news and entertainment
  • Write formal and informal communication
  • Participate in professional discussions
  • Demonstrate cultural understanding

Assessment ​

Assessment should measure understanding, not memorization.

Not: "Conjugate the verb regnen (to rain) in all tenses"

But: "Write 3 sentences about what the weather was like yesterday"

Why?

  • Shows real-world language ability
  • Measures understanding, not recall
  • More useful for learning
  • More engaging for learners
