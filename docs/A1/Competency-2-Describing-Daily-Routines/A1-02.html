Lesson A1-02: Describing Daily Routines ​

Level: A1 (Beginner) CEFR Level: A1 Duration: 90 minutes New Vocabulary: 30 words Grammar Focus: Review of present-tense conjugation with routine verbs, adverbs of frequency, time expressions with prepositions, sequencing and narrative adverbs

1. Lesson Goal ​

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to describe a complete daily routine in 6-10 simple sentences using correct present-tense forms, routine verbs, frequency adverbs, sequencing words, and time expressions.

2. Previous Knowledge ​

Before starting this lesson, you should have completed:

  • A1-01: Basic Sentences and Word Order (present-tense conjugation, V2 word order, question formation, negation)

You should be comfortable with:

  • The three main present-tense verb shapes: stem (ik), stem + -t (jij/u/hij/zij/het), infinitive (wij/jullie/zij)
  • Subject-verb agreement
  • Basic SVO word order and V2 inversion
  • Recognizing regular infinitives and forming their stems

3. Why This Matters ​

Describing your daily routine is one of the most practical uses of present-tense Dutch. In A1-01, you learned that verbs conjugate predictably, but you mostly practiced with short statements. Now you'll apply that knowledge to an extended routine: what you do in the morning, afternoon, and evening, how often you do things, and the order they happen. This is how you tell people about your life -- and it's the foundation for narrative, explanations, and social conversation.

4. Grammar ​

4.1 Review of Present Tense with Routine Verbs ​

The three-form present-tense system from A1-01 applies to all regular verbs, including routine verbs like ontbijten (have breakfast), douchen (shower), wandelen (walk), fietsen (cycle), sporten (exercise), and slapen (sleep). These verbs follow the same conjugation rule:

SubjectPatternExample (wandelen)English
ikstemIk wandel.I walk.
jij / jestem + -tJij wandelt.You walk.
ustem + -tU wandelt.You (formal) walk.
hijstem + -tHij wandelt.He walks.
zijstem + -tZij wandelt.She walks.
hetstem + -tHet wandelt.It walks.
wij / weinfinitiveWij wandelen.We walk.
jullieinfinitiveJullie wandelen.You (plural) walk.
zij / zeinfinitiveZij wandelen.They walk.

Three main shapes to remember:

  • Stem: Ik douche. (I shower.)
  • Stem + -t: Jij doucht. (You shower.) / Zij doucht. (She showers.)
  • Infinitive: Wij douchen. (We shower.) / Zij douchen. (They shower.)

Key point: The same rule works for all routine verbs. If you can conjugate "wandelen," you can conjugate "fietsen," "slapen," "koken," and any regular verb.

See: /shared/grammar/present-tense-conjugation.md for additional details and irregular verbs.

4.2 Adverbs of Frequency ​

Frequency adverbs answer the question "how often?" They tell whether an action is habitual, occasional, rare, or never happens. Common Dutch frequency adverbs include:

DutchEnglishUsage
altijdalwaysIk drink altijd thee. (I always drink tea.)
meestalusuallyIk ontbijt meestal om acht uur. (I usually have breakfast at eight.)
vaakoftenWij wandelen vaak na de lunch. (We often walk after lunch.)
somssometimesSoms werk ik 's avonds. (Sometimes I work in the evening.)
zeldenseldomZij sport zelden. (She seldom exercises.)
nooitneverIk drink nooit koffie. (I never drink coffee.)

Placement in neutral main clauses: In a statement without fronted adverbials, the frequency adverb comes right after the finite verb:

  • Ik ontbijt meestal om acht uur. (I usually have breakfast at eight.)
  • Zij sport vaak. (She often exercises.)
  • Wij eten zelden laat. (We seldom eat late.)
  • Ik drink nooit koffie. (I never drink coffee.)

Placement after fronted expressions: If a time phrase or sequence word starts the sentence, the frequency adverb still comes after the finite verb, in its normal position:

  • Elke ochtend drink ik meestal thee. (Every morning I usually drink tea.)

Important note: "Nooit" means "never" by itself. Do not combine it with "niet" -- Ik drink nooit koffie, not "Ik drink niet nooit koffie."

Common mistakes:

  • Ik meestal ontbijt → Ik ontbijt meestal. (Adverb placement)
  • Ik drink koffie altijd → Ik drink altijd koffie. (Adverb placement)
  • Ik drink niet nooit koffie → Ik drink nooit koffie. (No double negation with nooit)

4.3 Time Expressions with Prepositions ​

Routines are always tied to specific times. Dutch uses different prepositions for different time scales:

PrepositionUsageExamples
omExact clock timeom zeven uur (at seven o'clock), om half acht (at half past seven)
opDays of the week, datesop maandag (on Monday), op 15 juni (on June 15)
inMonths, seasons, larger time periods, parts of the dayin januari (in January), in de winter (in winter), in de ochtend (in the morning), in de middag (in the afternoon)
perRate or frequency per time unittwee keer per week (twice per week), drie keer per dag (three times per day)
elkeEvery [time unit]elke dag (every day), elke ochtend (every morning), elke maandag (every Monday)

Examples:

  • Ik ontbijt om zeven uur. (I have breakfast at seven o'clock.)
  • Op maandag fiets ik. (On Monday I cycle.)
  • In januari werk ik veel. (In January I work a lot.)
  • Ik sport twee keer per week. (I exercise twice per week.)
  • Elke ochtend drink ik thee. (Every morning I drink tea.)

Common mistakes:

  • op acht uur → om acht uur (use "om" for clock times, not "op")
  • in maandag → op maandag (use "op" for days, not "in")
  • op januari → in januari (use "in" for months, not "op")
  • per een week → per week (don't use "een" with "per")

4.4 Sequencing and Narrative Adverbs ​

When describing a routine, you need words that order events: first this, then that, later something else. These sequencing adverbs are critical for extended discourse:

DutchEnglishUsage
eerstfirstEerst ontbijt ik. (First I have breakfast.)
daarnathen, after thatDaarna douche ik. (Then I shower.)
laterlaterLater werk ik. (Later I work.)
vervolgensnext, subsequentlyVervolgens lunch ik. (Next I have lunch.)
tot slot / ten slottefinally, in the endTot slot slaap ik. (Finally I sleep.)
eindelijkfinally, at lastEindelijk ik begrijp. (Finally I understand.) -- use sparingly in routines

Word order with sequencing adverbs: When a sequencing adverb starts a main clause, it occupies first position and triggers the V2 (verb-second) rule. The finite verb comes immediately second:

  • Eerst ontbijt ik. (First I have breakfast.)
  • Daarna douche ik. (Then I shower.)
  • Later werk ik. (Later I work.)
  • Vervolgens lunch ik. (Next I have lunch.)
  • Tot slot slaap ik. (Finally I sleep.)

This is the same V2 inversion you learned in A1-01, applied to sequencing words.

Common mistakes:

  • Daarna ik douche → Daarna douche ik. (Verb must be second)
  • Eerst ik ontbijt → Eerst ontbijt ik. (Verb must be second)
  • Using "eindelijk" for every routine step; reserve it for emotional emphasis on finally reaching something long-awaited

4.5 Verb Forms for Routines ​

Dutch present tense covers both immediate actions ("I am working now") and habitual routines ("I work every day"). There is no separate habitual form, unlike some languages. Context words clarify whether an action is habitual or immediate:

  • Ik werk nu. (I am working now. -- immediate)
  • Ik werk elke dag. (I work every day. -- habitual)
  • Zij fietst vaak. (She often cycles. -- habitual)
  • Wij lunchen om twaalf uur. (We have lunch at twelve. -- habitual, understood as daily routine)

In routine descriptions, these context clues (elke dag, meestal, vaak, time markers like om acht uur) tell the listener or reader that you're talking about something repeated, not something happening right now.

Common mistakes:

  • Ik ben werkend for habitual actions → Ik werk. (Dutch has no separate habitual form)
  • Overusing the helper verb "doen": Ik doe douchen → Ik douche. (Just use the verb directly)

5. Grammar Reasoning ​

Why Frequency Adverbs Come After the Verb ​

In English, we say "I usually work" with the adverb before the main verb. Dutch does the opposite: Ik werk meestal. This reflects the V2 principle: the finite verb occupies position 2, and most adverbials come after. It's not a quirk; it's the same rule that puts the verb second in any main clause.

Why Dutch Has Different Time Prepositions ​

English gets by with mostly "at" and "on," but Dutch distinguishes clock time (om), days (op), and larger periods or parts of day (in). This specificity is typical of European languages and reflects how speakers naturally chunk time: "at 8" (precise moment), "on Monday" (a day), "in the morning" (a part of the day). Learning these prepositions means thinking about time the Dutch way.

Why Sequencing Words Trigger V2 ​

When you start a sentence with "daarna" (then) or "eerst" (first), Dutch treats it as a fronted adverbial, just like a time phrase. The verb comes second. This gives routines a natural, flowing rhythm: "Daarna douche ik. Vervolgens lunch ik." Without this rule, you'd have to say "Ik douche daarna," which feels choppy.

7. Pronunciation ​

Routine Verbs ​

  • opstaan: OP-stahn (stress on first syllable)
  • wakker worden: VAH-ker VOR-duhn (both words stressed)
  • ontbijten: ont-BY-tuhn (stress on second syllable, long 'i' sound like English "ee")
  • lunchen: LUN-chuh (stress on first syllable, 'ch' like in "Bach")
  • koken: KOH-kuhn (long 'o' sound)
  • douchen: DOO-shuh (long 'oo' sound, like "ooze")
  • tanden poetsen: TAHN-duhn POOT-suhn (stress on first syllable of each word)
  • wandelen: VAHN-duh-luhn (stress on first syllable)
  • fietsen: FEET-suhn (long 'ee' sound)
  • sporten: SPOR-tuhn (stress on first syllable, short 'o')

Time-Related Words ​

  • ochtend: OKH-tunt (stress on first syllable, guttural 'ch' sound)
  • middag: MIH-dakh (stress on first syllable, short 'i')
  • avond: AH-vont (stress on first syllable, short 'a')
  • nacht: nahkt (stress on first syllable, guttural 'ch')

Frequency Adverbs ​

  • altijd: AHL-tait (stress on first syllable)
  • meestal: MAYS-tahl (stress on first syllable, long 'ay' sound)
  • vaak: vahk (long 'aa' sound, like in "father")
  • soms: soms (short 'o')
  • zelden: ZEL-duhn (stress on first syllable)
  • nooit: noy-t (diphthong like English "boy")

Sequencing Adverbs ​

  • eerst: ayrst (long 'ay' sound, like in "fair")
  • daarna: DAHR-nah (stress on first syllable, long 'aa' sound)
  • later: LAH-ter (stress on first syllable)
  • vervolgens: vuhr-VOL-khuhns (stress on second syllable, guttural sounds)
  • tot slot: tot slot (both words, stress on first syllables)

8. Dialogue ​

Dialogue 1: Morning Routine ​

A: Wanneer word je wakker? B: Ik word om zeven uur wakker. Daarna douche ik. Ik ontbijt meestal om half acht. A: Eet je altijd ontbijt? B: Ja, ik eet altijd ontbijt. Later werk ik.

English Translation:A: When do you wake up? B: I wake up at seven. Then I shower. I usually have breakfast at half past seven. A: Do you always eat breakfast? B: Yes, I always eat breakfast. Later I work.

Dialogue 2: Afternoon Routine ​

A: Wat doe je in de middag? B: Ik werk meestal tot twaalf uur. Om twaalf uur lunch ik. Later wandel ik. A: Loop je elke dag? B: Nee, ik wandel soms. Op maandag fiets ik.

English Translation:A: What do you do in the afternoon? B: I usually work until twelve. At twelve I have lunch. Later I walk. A: Do you walk every day? B: No, I sometimes walk. On Monday I cycle.

Dialogue 3: Evening Routine ​

A: Wat doe je 's avonds? B: Eerst kook ik. Daarna eet ik avondeten. Tot slot slaap ik. A: Hoe laat ga je slapen? B: Ik slaap meestal om elf uur.

English Translation:A: What do you do in the evening? B: First I cook. Then I eat dinner. Finally I sleep. A: What time do you go to bed? B: I usually go to bed at eleven.

Dialogue 4: Frequency Habits ​

A: Sport je vaak? B: Nee, ik sport zelden. Twee keer per maand. A: Fiets je meer? B: Ja, ik fiets elke dag op maandag en woensdag.

English Translation:A: Do you exercise often? B: No, I seldom exercise. Twice a month. A: Do you cycle more? B: Yes, I cycle every Monday and Wednesday.

Dialogue 5: Weekday Variation ​

A: Werk je op zaterdag? B: Nee. Op maandag tot vrijdag werk ik. Op zaterdag rust ik. A: Wat doe je op zondag? B: Op zondag wandel ik meestal en ik cook voor mezelf.

English Translation:A: Do you work on Saturday? B: No. Monday through Friday I work. On Saturday I rest. A: What do you do on Sunday? B: On Sunday I usually walk and I cook for myself.

9. Reading ​

Routine Text ​

Maria beschrijft haar dagelijkse routine. Maria woont in Amsterdam en werkt als docent.

Maria staat elke ochtend om zeven uur op. Daarna doucht ze en ze poets haar tanden. Om half acht ontbijt Maria. Ze drinkt meestal thee en eet brood.

Om negen uur werk Maria. Ze werkt in een school. Tot twaalf uur onderwijs Maria kinderen. Dan lunch Maria in de school. Ze eet meestal een boterham met kaas.

Na de lunch wandelt Maria. Ze wandelt graag in het park. Later werkt zij weer. Om vier uur gaat Maria naar huis.

's Avonds kookt Maria voor zichzelf. Ze eet avondeten om zes uur. Na het eten rust Maria. Ze kijkt naar televisie of leest een boek. Tot slot gaat Maria om elf uur slapen.

Op zaterdag werkt Maria niet. Ze wandelt veel, fietst soms en bezoekt vrienden. Op zondag rust Maria helemaal.

English Translation:

Maria describes her daily routine. Maria lives in Amsterdam and works as a teacher.

Maria gets up every morning at seven. Then she showers and brushes her teeth. At half past seven Maria has breakfast. She usually drinks tea and eats bread.

At nine Maria works. She works in a school. Until twelve Maria teaches children. Then Maria has lunch at the school. She usually eats a sandwich with cheese.

After lunch Maria walks. She likes to walk in the park. Later she works again. At four Maria goes home.

In the evening Maria cooks for herself. She eats dinner at six. After eating Maria rests. She watches television or reads a book. Finally Maria goes to bed at eleven.

On Saturday Maria does not work. She walks a lot, sometimes cycles, and visits friends. On Sunday Maria rests completely.

Reading Comprehension Questions ​

  1. Hoe laat staat Maria op? (What time does Maria get up?)

    • Answer: Maria staat om zeven uur op. (Maria gets up at seven.)

  2. Wat doet Maria na het douchen? (What does Maria do after showering?)

    • Answer: Zij poets haar tanden en ontbijt. (She brushes her teeth and has breakfast.)

  3. Waar werkt Maria? (Where does Maria work?)

    • Answer: Maria werkt in een school. (Maria works in a school.)

  4. Wat doet Maria na de lunch? (What does Maria do after lunch?)

    • Answer: Maria wandelt in het park. (Maria walks in the park.)

  5. Hoe laat gaat Maria slapen? (What time does Maria go to sleep?)

    • Answer: Maria gaat om elf uur slapen. (Maria goes to bed at eleven.)

  6. Sport Maria op zaterdag? (Does Maria exercise on Saturday?)

    • Answer: Maria wandelt en fietst soms op zaterdag. (Maria walks and sometimes cycles on Saturday.)

10. Listening ​

Listening Text (Transcript) ​

Interview with Peter about his routine

Interviewer: Goedemorgen Peter. Kunt u uw dagelijkse routine beschrijven?

Peter: Ja, goed. Ik sta elke ochtend om zes uur op. Ik douche meteen en ik poets mijn tanden. Om half zeven ontbijt ik. Ik eet altijd brood en ik drink koffie.

Interviewer: Hoe laat gaat u naar het werk?

Peter: Om zeven uur ga ik naar het werk. Ik werk op kantoor. Ik werk meestal tot vijf uur. Dan ga ik naar huis.

Interviewer: Wat doet u na het werk?

Peter: In de avond wandel ik soms. Ik cook meestal. Om zes uur eet ik avondeten. Daarna rust ik. Ik kijk naar sport op televisie.

Interviewer: Wanneer gaat u slapen?

English Translation:

Interviewer: Good morning Peter. Can you describe your daily routine?

Peter: Yes, of course. I get up every morning at six. I shower immediately and I brush my teeth. At half past six I have breakfast. I always eat bread and I drink coffee.

Interviewer: What time do you go to work?

Peter: At seven I go to work. I work in an office. I usually work until five. Then I go home.

Interviewer: What do you do after work?

Peter: In the evening I sometimes walk. I usually cook. At six I eat dinner. After that I rest. I watch sports on television.

Interviewer: When do you go to sleep?

Peter: I usually sleep at ten. On Saturday and Sunday I go to bed late -- at eleven or half past eleven.

  2. Wat eet Peter bij het ontbijt? (What does Peter eat for breakfast?)

    • Answer: Peter eet brood en drinkt koffie. (Peter eats bread and drinks coffee.)

  3. Waar werkt Peter? (Where does Peter work?)

    • Answer: Peter werkt op kantoor. (Peter works in an office.)

  4. Wat doet Peter in de avond? (What does Peter do in the evening?)

    • Answer: Peter wandelt soms, cooks meestal en kijkt naar sport. (Peter sometimes walks, usually cooks, and watches sports.)

    • Answer: Op zaterdag gaat Peter om elf uur of half twaalf slapen. (On Saturday Peter goes to bed at eleven or half past eleven.)

13. Common Mistakes ​

Mistake 1: Wrong Conjugation with "Ik" ​

Error: Ik werken / Ik douchen Correction: Ik werk / Ik douche Explanation: The subject "ik" uses the stem, not the infinitive. Only wij/jullie/zij (plural) use the infinitive.

Mistake 2: Missing or Extra -t with "Jij" ​

Error 1: Jij werken → Jij werkt Error 2: Werkt jij? → Werk jij? Explanation: In statements, jij takes -t (jij werkt). In questions with verb-inversion, the -t drops: Werk jij? (not Werkt jij?).

Mistake 3: Plural Subject Taking -t ​

Error: Wij sport / Zij ducht Correction: Wij sporten / Zij douchen Explanation: Plural subjects (wij, jullie, zij) use the infinitive form, never -t.

Mistake 4: Wrong Preposition for Time ​

Error 1: Ik ontbijt op acht uur. → Ik ontbijt om acht uur. (use "om" for clock times) Error 2: In maandag fiets ik. → Op maandag fiets ik. (use "op" for days) Error 3: Op januari → In januari (use "in" for months)

Mistake 5: Adverb in Wrong Position ​

Error: Ik meestal ontbijt / Ik ontbijt thee meestal → Ik ontbijt meestal. Correction: Ik ontbijt meestal. Explanation: The frequency adverb comes right after the finite verb, not at the beginning or end.

Mistake 6: Double Negation with "Nooit" ​

Error: Ik drink niet nooit koffie. Correction: Ik drink nooit koffie. Explanation: "Nooit" already means "never"; do not combine it with "niet."

Mistake 7: Wrong Word Order After Sequencing Adverb ​

Error: Daarna ik douche. Correction: Daarna douche ik. Explanation: Sequencing adverbs trigger the V2 (verb-second) rule. The verb comes second, not after the subject.

14. Dutch Insight ​

Dutch Routines and Directness ​

Dutch people tend to describe routines with precision and directness. Notice that in the dialogues and examples, speakers state exactly what time they do things and how often. There's no vagueness like English "I usually get up around 7" -- Dutch speakers typically say "Ik sta om zeven uur op" (I get up at seven o'clock). This reflects Dutch culture's emphasis on punctuality, planning, and efficiency.

Also notice the frequent use of sequencing adverbs in extended routines. Dutch values clarity and logical flow; speakers chain events with "eerst...daarna...later...tot slot" to make the routine easy to follow. This structure is deeply embedded in Dutch communication style.

15. Memory Tricks ​

Trick 1: The Three Shapes ​

Remember the three present-tense shapes by thinking of three groups:

  • Group 1 (stem): Ik (just the stem, bare and simple)
  • Group 2 (stem + -t): Jij/U/Hij/Zij/Het (the formal or "other people" group, all get -t)
  • Group 3 (infinitive): Wij/Jullie/Zij (plural/collective, keeps the infinitive)

Trick 2: Om/Op/In for Time ​

  • Om = O'clock (Exact clock time: om zeven uur)
  • Op = On (Days: op maandag)
  • In = In (Months/seasons/parts of day: in januari, in de ochtend)

Trick 3: Frequency Adverb Placement ​

"Frequency follows the verb" -- The adverb comes right after the finite verb in main clauses. If you can place an adverb in English after the verb (in colloquial speech), Dutch does it as the standard rule:

  • English (informal): "I usually eat lunch." → Dutch: "Ik eet meestal lunch."

Trick 4: Sequencing Adverbs Trigger V2 ​

Think of sequencing adverbs (Eerst, Daarna, Later, Vervolgens, Tot slot) as "fronted adverbials" that demand verb-second word order, just like time phrases:

  • Eerst [verb] [subject]...
  • Daarna [verb] [subject]...
  • Later [verb] [subject]...

Trick 5: The jij -t Drop ​

When you turn a jij statement into a question, drop the -t:

  • Statement: Jij werkt. (You work.)
  • Question: Werk jij? (Do you work?)
  • Memory: "Question reversal drops the -t."

19. Summary ​

What You Learned ​

In this lesson, you practiced describing daily routines using:

  1. Regular present-tense verb conjugation -- The three-form system (stem for ik, stem + -t for third person singular, infinitive for plural) applied productively to routine verbs like douchen, wandelen, koken, and fietsen.

  2. Frequency adverbs (altijd, meestal, vaak, soms, zelden, nooit) -- Placed directly after the finite verb in neutral statements to indicate how often an action happens.

  3. Time expressions with correct prepositions -- Om for clock time (om zeven uur), op for days (op maandag), in for months/seasons/parts of day (in januari, in de ochtend), per for rates (twee keer per week), and elke for "every" (elke dag).

  4. Sequencing and narrative adverbs (eerst, daarna, later, vervolgens, tot slot) -- Used at the beginning of clauses to order routine events; these trigger V2 word order (verb-second).

  5. Extended discourse -- Moving from single sentences to 6-10 sentence routines, connecting ideas with sequencing words and time markers.
