# Research Request: A0-01 - Understanding Dutch Sounds and Spelling

## Competency

**Name:** Understanding Dutch Sounds and Spelling  
**CEFR Level:** A0  
**Lesson ID:** A0-01

## Competency Description

The learner can:
- Read the Dutch alphabet aloud
- Distinguish between long and short vowel sounds
- Recognize and pronounce diphthongs (ui, ou, au, ei, ij, etc.)
- Identify word stress patterns
- Read simple Dutch words with reasonable pronunciation
- Understand basic spelling rules

## What We Need for This Lesson

For the A0-01 lesson, please research and provide:

### 1. Grammar Concepts
- Explain the Dutch alphabet (A-Z) with pronunciation guidance
- Explain long vs. short vowel distinction with examples
- Explain diphthongs (ui, ou, au, ei, ij) with examples
- Explain word stress patterns and how they affect pronunciation
- Explain why Dutch spelling and pronunciation differ from English

**Format:** For each concept, provide:
- Clear explanation (as if teaching an English speaker)
- Why it matters
- 3-5 examples with Dutch words and English approximation of pronunciation
- Common mistakes English speakers make

### 2. Vocabulary
25-35 words that serve as phonetic teaching examples:
- Alphabet letter names (a, b, c, etc.)
- Examples of short vowels (words showing a, e, i, o, u in closed syllables)

- Examples of diphthongs (ui, ou, au, ei, ij)
- Examples of Dutch-specific consonant sounds (g, h, r, w)
- Examples showing word stress patterns

**For each word, provide:**
- Dutch spelling
- English translation/meaning
- Article (de, het, or -- if not a noun)
- Plural form (or -- if not applicable)
- English approximation of pronunciation (e.g., MAHN, not [man])
- Part of speech
- One example sentence in Dutch
- English translation of the example

### 3. Example Sentences and Dialogues

Provide 5-8 short example sentences/dialogues showing:
- Pronunciation in context
- How to read Dutch aloud
- Spelling-pronunciation patterns

**Format:** Dutch sentence → English pronunciation guide → English meaning

### 4. Cultural Notes

- Why Dutch pronunciation can be difficult for English speakers
- How Dutch spelling conventions differ from English
- Any regional pronunciation variations learners should know about

### 5. Exercises (Describe What Should Be Covered)

What types of exercises should this lesson include? Examples:

- Reading aloud practice

**For each exercise type:**
- What skill it targets

### 6. Assessment Criteria

How will we know if a learner has mastered this lesson? Describe:

## Guidelines

- All pronunciation should be in English approximation (no IPA symbols)
- Mark stress with UPPERCASE letters (e.g., MAHN, KAH-mer)
- Assume the learner is a complete beginner with no Dutch knowledge
- Avoid linguistic jargon unless you define it
- Focus on practical pronunciation guidance
- Examples should be real Dutch words, not made-up
- Keep language simple and clear
