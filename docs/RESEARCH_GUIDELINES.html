Research Guidelines for Dutch B1 Curriculum ​

When you conduct external research for lesson content, you will encounter clarifying questions from your research source. This document provides standing answers to the most common questions so you get consistent, high-quality output every time.

Common Clarifying Questions & Standing Answers ​

Question 1: Should I focus on Netherlands Dutch (NL) or include Belgian Dutch (Flemish)? ​

Answer: Focus strictly on Netherlands Dutch (Standard Dutch / Standaardnederlands).

  • Why: The curriculum is designed for learners in/interacting with the Netherlands.
  • Exception: Cultural notes may mention that Belgian Dutch exists (e.g., "In Belgium, the word X is pronounced Y"), but this is secondary.
  • Spelling: Use Netherlands spelling standards (e.g., "nummers" not "numers").
  • Pronunciation: Netherlands accent/pronunciation only.

You can say: "Netherlands Dutch only, please. This is for learners in/connecting with the Netherlands. Cultural notes can mention Belgian differences briefly, but focus on NL."

Question 2: Do you want pronunciation approximations only, or also brief teaching tips for pronunciation? ​

Answer: Both—approximations (English-style) AND brief teaching tips.

For Approximations:

  • Use English approximations only (no IPA symbols)
  • Mark stressed syllables with UPPERCASE
  • Examples: MAAN-dag (Monday), FEB-roo-ah-ree (February), KRAAR-oo-ver (quarter past)

For Teaching Tips:

  • Include 1-2 sentence guidance per sound if it differs significantly from English
  • Example: "The Dutch 'ch' is like Spanish 'jota' (guttural), not English 'ch' in 'church'"
  • Focus on sounds that are hard for English speakers

You can say: "Please include English pronunciation approximations (with UPPERCASE stress marks) AND brief teaching tips (1-2 sentences) for Dutch sounds that differ from English. E.g., approximation: KRAAR-oo-ver, tip: The 'aa' is like English 'ah' but held slightly longer."

Question 3: Should the content be concise for lesson integration or more expanded for teacher reference? ​

Answer: Concise for lesson integration, but complete and reference-ready.

Be concise on:

  • Grammar explanations (2-3 paragraphs per concept, not textbook chapters)
  • Dialogue length (2-3 exchanges per dialogue, not full conversations)
  • Exercise descriptions (enough detail to create a lesson, not elaborate scaffolding)

Be complete on:

  • All required sections (no skipping or abbreviating required fields)
  • Vocabulary table (every word needs all 8 columns)
  • Example sentences (full Dutch + English, not abbreviated)
  • Assessment criteria (clear, measurable standards)

You can say: "Keep explanations concise but complete—2-3 paragraphs per grammar topic, 2-3 exchanges per dialogue. But include all required sections and fields. This is for direct lesson integration, not supplementary teacher guides."

Question 4: Anything else I should know about your curriculum? ​

Answer: Provide these key constraints:

  1. One new grammar concept per lesson maximum
  2. 20-35 new vocabulary words per lesson
  3. All examples must use ONLY previously taught + current-lesson vocabulary/grammar (no preview of future lessons)
  4. Days, months, seasons are case-sensitive (lowercase in running text, uppercase only in headings/formal lists in Dutch)
  5. Cultural notes should help English speakers understand Dutch context (punctuality, holidays, formatting conventions, etc.)
  6. All dialogue must sound natural and practical (reflecting real Dutch life: work, school, appointments, seasonal activities)
  7. For A0-06 specifically: Days/months/seasons are the primary focus; time prepositions (om, op, in) are secondary; frequency expressions (elke dag, elke week) are brief introduction

You can say: "A few constraints: (1) One grammar concept per lesson max, (2) 20-35 new vocab words, (3) Examples use ONLY taught or current-lesson vocabulary—no future lessons, (4) Dutch capitalization rules matter for days/months (lowercase in running text), (5) Cultural notes should help English speakers understand Dutch context, (6) Keep dialogue natural and practical."

Please use these parameters:

  1. Dutch variant: Netherlands Dutch (Standard Dutch / Standaardnederlands) only
  2. Pronunciation: English approximations only (no IPA); mark stressed syllables in UPPERCASE; include 1-2 sentence teaching tips if the sound differs significantly from English
  3. Content scope: Concise but complete—2-3 paragraphs per grammar topic, all required fields filled, no abbreviated sections
  4. Key constraints:
    • One grammar concept per lesson max
    • 20-35 new vocabulary words

    • Dialogue must sound natural and practical (real Dutch life)
    • For A0-06: Days/months/seasons are primary; time prepositions (om, op, in) are secondary; frequency (elke dag, elke week) is brief intro
