CEFR Guide ​

CEFR = Common European Framework of Reference for Languages. This is the standard used across Europe to describe language proficiency.

This document defines the educational scope of each CEFR level within the Dutch B1 Curriculum. It specifies learning outcomes, grammar scope, vocabulary scope, communication skills, reading, listening, speaking, writing, and assessment expectations.

No lesson may introduce concepts outside the scope defined here. If a grammar point or vocabulary topic is not listed for a level (or an earlier level), it does not belong in that level's lessons.

See: docs/CURRICULUM_PRINCIPLES.md for how this scope is turned into competencies, units, and lessons.

Course Overview ​

A0 (Foundations) -> A1 (Beginner) -> A2 (Elementary) -> B1 (Independent User)

Each level prepares the learner for the next. No level's lessons may assume grammar or vocabulary that belongs to a later level.

A0 -- Foundations ​

Goal ​

Build confidence. Understand how Dutch works. Develop correct pronunciation. Learn to create very simple sentences. No prior knowledge is assumed.

Exit Profile ​

The learner should be able to:

  • Read Dutch aloud with reasonable pronunciation.
  • Understand the Dutch alphabet and spelling.
  • Introduce themselves.
  • Ask and answer simple personal questions.
  • Form basic present tense sentences.
  • Recognize the most common sentence patterns.
  • Understand approximately 300-500 high-frequency words.

Grammar Scope ​

  • Alphabet
  • Pronunciation (long/short vowels, diphthongs, stress)
  • Articles (de / het)
  • Singular nouns
  • Personal pronouns
  • Present tense (regular verbs)
  • Basic irregular verbs (zijn, hebben)
  • Simple questions
  • Negation (niet / geen)
  • Basic word order
  • Numbers
  • Days and months
  • Possessive pronouns
  • Demonstratives (this/that)
  • Simple adjectives
  • Yes/No questions

Vocabulary Scope ​

Greetings, introductions, family, numbers, colours, time, days, months, countries, nationalities, jobs, home, classroom, daily objects, food basics, drinks, weather basics, common verbs.

Target: 300-500 words

Communication ​

The learner can greet people, introduce themselves, say where they live, describe family, order simple food, ask simple questions, and answer predictable questions.

Reading ​

Short notices, menus, signs, simple dialogues.

Listening ​

Very slow speech, clearly articulated sentences, predictable conversations.

Speaking ​

Simple memorized conversations, short answers, simple descriptions.

Writing ​

Personal information, very short messages, simple sentences, forms.

A1 -- Beginner ​

Goal ​

Become comfortable handling everyday situations.

Exit Profile ​

The learner should be able to participate in short conversations, understand simple spoken Dutch, describe daily life, write simple messages, and read short texts.

Vocabulary: 800-1,000 words (cumulative)

Grammar Scope ​

Everything from A0, plus:

  • Plurals
  • Diminutives
  • Modal verbs
  • Separable verbs
  • Reflexive verbs
  • Present continuous concepts
  • Adjective agreement
  • Comparative
  • Superlative
  • Prepositions of place
  • Time expressions
  • Word order after adverbs
  • Imperative
  • Introduction to perfect tense

Vocabulary Scope ​

Shopping, house, furniture, transportation, school, work, leisure, sports, clothes, health basics, restaurants, holidays, directions, technology basics.

Target: 800-1,000 words total

Communication ​

The learner can shop, travel, ask for directions, visit a doctor, talk about hobbies, describe routines, make appointments, and talk about likes and dislikes.

Reading ​

Simple articles, advertisements, instructions, emails.

Listening ​

Daily conversations, announcements, short interviews.

Speaking ​

Role plays, simple storytelling, asking for information.

Writing ​

Emails, invitations, diary entries, descriptions.

A2 -- Elementary ​

Goal ​

Become independent in familiar situations.

Exit Profile ​

The learner should be able to maintain everyday conversations, understand routine interactions, explain experiences, describe future plans, and express opinions.

Vocabulary: 1,800-2,000 words (cumulative)

Grammar Scope ​

Everything from A1, plus:

  • Perfect tense
  • Past tense
  • Future tense
  • Passive introduction
  • Relative clauses
  • Subordinate clauses
  • Conjunctions
  • Word order inversion
  • Indefinite pronouns
  • Object pronouns
  • More complex prepositions

Vocabulary Scope ​

Healthcare, municipality, banking, employment, housing, education, parenting, travel, nature, media, technology, emergencies, government.

Target: 1,800-2,000 words total

Communication ​

The learner can explain problems, tell stories, describe experiences, discuss plans, handle official situations, make complaints, and give advice.

Reading ​

Newspapers, brochures, instructions, short stories, official letters.

Listening ​

Native conversations, news summaries, telephone calls, meetings.

Speaking ​

Longer conversations, storytelling, problem solving, expressing opinions.

Writing ​

Formal emails, complaints, personal letters, instructions.

B1 -- Independent User ​

Goal ​

Function independently in Dutch society.

Exit Profile ​

The learner should be able to live comfortably in the Netherlands, participate in workplace conversations, understand most daily spoken Dutch, express opinions confidently, and pass a Dutch B1 examination.

Vocabulary: 2,800-3,200 words (cumulative)

Grammar Scope ​

Everything from A2, plus:

  • Advanced subordinate clauses
  • Reported speech (introductory)
  • Passive voice
  • Conditional sentences
  • Complex conjunctions
  • Nuanced modal usage
  • Idiomatic expressions
  • Formal vs informal register

Vocabulary Scope ​

Politics, news, workplace, business, finance, society, culture, environment, digital life, education, law basics, public services.

Target: 2,800-3,200 words total

Communication ​

The learner can debate familiar topics, explain opinions, negotiate, solve problems, participate in meetings, deliver presentations, and understand everyday media.

Reading ​

Newspapers, websites, official documents, short novels, workplace documents.

Listening ​

Native-speed conversations, podcasts, television, meetings, radio.

Speaking ​

Discussions, presentations, negotiations, storytelling, opinions.

Writing ​

Reports, formal emails, applications, complaints, summaries, opinion pieces.

Review Strategy ​

Each level contains Unit Reviews, a Mid-Level Review, a Final Review, and a Level Assessment. No review introduces new content. See: docs/REVIEW_STRATEGY.md.

Assessment Philosophy ​

Assessment measures communication rather than memorization. Every assessment evaluates reading, listening, speaking, writing, grammar in context, and vocabulary in context.

A1 Exam ​

Listening (simple conversations), reading (simple texts), writing (a simple email or postcard), speaking (a simple conversation).

B1 Exam ​

Listening (conversations on familiar topics), reading (articles and emails), writing (an essay or letter), speaking (discussing opinions and experiences).

Transition Criteria ​

A learner should only advance after demonstrating confidence in the previous level's communication goals, not simply completing all lessons. Mastery is defined by the ability to understand, produce, and apply the language in realistic situations.

How This Scope Becomes Lessons ​

This document defines what belongs in each level. It intentionally does not define how many units or lessons each level will contain, and it is not turned directly into a lesson count.

The curriculum is not sized by picking a target number of lessons per level and filling it. It is sized by first defining the competencies a learner needs (see docs/COMPETENCY_MAP.md), then letting the number of units and lessons fall out of what each competency actually requires. A competency that needs 3 lessons gets 3; one that needs 8 gets 8. Level size is a byproduct of scope, never the starting point.

Using CEFR Levels ​

When creating content, always note the CEFR level in the metadata header:

**CEFR Level:** A1
**Duration:** 60 minutes
**Vocabulary Items:** 25
**Grammar Topics:** Personal pronouns, present tense of "to be"

This helps learners understand where they are and where they are going.
